# YC RFS Reference Snapshot

Source: `https://www.ycombinator.com/rfs`
Captured: 2026-05-03
Current visible section: Summer 2026

Important caveat from YC: RFS is a tradition of sharing ideas YC would like founders to tackle. YC says these ideas are only a fraction of what they fund; founders do not need to work on an RFS idea to apply.

## Summer 2026 Meta Theme

AI has moved from feature to foundation. YC is looking for startups rebuilding software, services, silicon, and physical-world systems around AI.

## Current RFS Themes

### AI for Low-Pesticide Agriculture

AI vision, cheap sensors/cameras, robotics, biology, and precision treatment can reduce pesticide use while increasing yield. Strong ideas combine real-world perception, robotics/biology, and a large economic wedge.

### AI-Native Discovery Engines

Scientific discovery is shifting from human-heavy hypothesis/experiment loops to AI-native closed loops. Strong ideas go beyond copilots and run design-make-test-analyze systems with researchers.

### AI-Native Service Companies

YC is interested in companies that sell the service, not software tools. Target outsourced service spend where AI can do the work directly. Examples include insurance brokerage, accounting/tax/audit, compliance, and healthcare administration.

### AI Personalized Medicine

Agents plus falling diagnostic/genomic/therapy costs enable personalized care and treatments. Strong ideas use abundant personal data and intelligence to support diagnosis, risk, treatment, or care delivery.

### Company Brain

The AI automation blocker is domain knowledge scattered across company systems and people. Strong ideas structure company know-how into a living executable map or skills file that lets AI do work safely and consistently.

### Counter-Swarm Defense

Drone defense is becoming a realtime distributed-systems problem. Strong ideas restore cost advantage to defenders by fusing sensors/defenders, neutralizing swarms, or attacking autonomy stacks.

### Dynamic Software Interfaces

Coding agents make software interfaces customizable by users. Strong ideas rethink software delivery around shared primitives that agents/users can modify, not one-size-fits-all UIs.

### Electronics in Space

Reusable rockets increase the need for space compute, especially inference chips optimized for mass, thermal, and radiation constraints.

### Hardware Supply Chain

Hardware iteration outside Shenzhen is too slow. Strong ideas produce parts faster, enable rapid hardware iteration, or integrate design/manufacturing/logistics tightly.

### Industrial Capabilities in Space

YC is interested in moon/space industrial capability: extracting raw materials and manufacturing structures from local materials.

### Inference Chips for Agent Workflows

Agent workloads loop through tool calls, branching, context, memory, and orchestration. Strong chip ideas optimize for agent execution graphs, context switching, KV cache persistence, and compilers/runtime, not just prompt-response inference.

### SaaS Challengers

AI reduces software-building cost by 10-100x, creating a chance to attack legacy SaaS. Strong ideas rethink workflows AI-natively, bundle point solutions, open-source expensive incumbents, or go after large hard categories.

### Software for Agents

Future users include AI agents. Strong ideas build machine-readable APIs, MCPs, CLIs, docs, onboarding, and agent-first software rather than visual browser workflows for humans only.

### Startups That Want to Sell to Huge Companies

AI makes massive enterprises more reachable and lets small teams ship deep useful products quickly. Strong ideas can land serious pilots/deals with F100-scale companies by solving urgent AI-era problems.

### Supply Chain 2.0 for Semiconductors

AI chip supply chains are complex, constrained, spreadsheet-heavy, and exposed to export/compliance risk. Strong ideas bring realtime allocation tracking, multi-tier risk monitoring, and supply-chain visibility.

### The AI Operating System for Companies

AI-native companies make themselves queryable and closed-loop. Strong ideas connect Slack, Linear, GitHub, Notion, call recordings, and product/engineering/customer artifacts into an intelligence layer that can reason, flag drift, and generate specs agents can execute.

## LiveLabs-Relevant RFS Mappings

### StreamBridge / Stream

Strongest mappings:
- **Dynamic Software Interfaces**: generated event pages and producer surfaces from shared live primitives.
- **Software for Agents**: Director and event infrastructure exposed as tools/skills/APIs for agentic production.
- **The AI Operating System for Companies**: if framed as live-event operations becoming queryable, monitorable, and spec-generating.
- **Company Brain**: if positioned for Chumo/institutions as encoded event/webinar/CPD operating knowledge.
- **SaaS Challengers**: if attacking OBS/vMix/LiveU/event-platform workflows with AI-native live production.
- **Startups That Want to Sell to Huge Companies**: if targeting enterprises/institutions that need repeatable live operations.

Weaker but interesting mappings:
- **Counter-Swarm Defense**: only by analogy: realtime distributed sensor/camera fusion. Do not overclaim.
- **Inference Chips for Agent Workflows**: only relevant if Director/LiveKit workloads expose agent execution constraints at scale.

### Chumo

Strongest mappings:
- **AI-Native Service Companies**: if Chumo sells managed institute/event operations, not just software.
- **Company Brain**: institute know-how, CPD workflows, compliance, certificates, event runbooks encoded into skills.
- **The AI Operating System for Companies**: recurring institute operations turned into a closed-loop system.
- **SaaS Challengers**: replacing webinar/LMS/event tooling bundles with an AI-native workflow.

## YC-Style Validation Questions

- Is AI the foundation, or just a feature?
- Does this replace the old workflow, not merely improve a step?
- What changed in the last 12-24 months that makes this possible now?
- Who is desperate enough to use a rough version?
- Can a tiny team ship something surprisingly deep in months?
- Is the demo obvious in minutes?
- Does the system learn or compound with every customer/event?
- Does the founder have unfair insight or access?
